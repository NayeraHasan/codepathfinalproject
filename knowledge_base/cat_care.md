# Cat Care Guide

## Feeding Guidelines

Cats are obligate carnivores and require a protein-rich diet.
- Kittens (under 1 year): 3–4 small meals daily.
- Adult cats (1–7 years): 2 meals daily.
- Senior cats (7+ years): 2–3 smaller meals daily.

Recommended schedule:
- Morning: 7:00 AM – 8:00 AM
- Evening: 5:00 PM – 6:00 PM

Cats have a naturally low thirst drive; wet food (≈80% water) helps supplement daily fluid intake. Always keep fresh water available; a circulating water fountain encourages drinking.

## Litter Box Maintenance

- Scoop at least once daily; twice daily is ideal for multi-cat households.
- Full litter change every 1–2 weeks for clumping litter.
- Wash the box with mild unscented soap monthly.
- General rule: one litter box per cat, plus one extra.
- Place boxes in quiet, accessible locations away from food and water.

## Play and Enrichment

Cats are crepuscular (most active at dawn and dusk):
- 2–3 interactive play sessions of 10–15 minutes each day.
- Best times: morning and evening.
- Rotate toys weekly to prevent boredom.
- Provide at least one scratching post per cat to reduce furniture damage and support claw health.

## Health and Medications

- **Flea prevention**: Monthly topical treatment (Revolution, Frontline) — even indoor cats benefit.
- **Vaccines**: FVRCP core vaccine every 1–3 years; rabies per local law; FeLV recommended for outdoor cats.
- **Dental care**: Brush teeth 2–3 times weekly if the cat tolerates it; dental treats can help between brushings.
- **Deworming**: As directed by your veterinarian based on lifestyle and risk.

## Grooming

- Short-haired cats: brush weekly to reduce shedding and hairballs.
- Long-haired cats: brush daily to prevent matting.
- Check ears weekly for dark debris (possible ear mites) or redness.
- Trim nails every 2–3 weeks.

## Warning Signs — Seek Veterinary Care

- Not eating for more than 24 hours
- Hiding more than usual or sudden behavior change
- Changes in litter box habits (straining, blood, or going outside the box)
- Excessive grooming, hair loss, or skin sores
- Labored or open-mouth breathing (always an emergency in cats)
