# Pet Medication Management

## Common Medications by Species

### Dogs

**Heartworm Prevention**
- Frequency: Monthly oral tablet (same calendar day each month)
- Common products: Heartgard, Interceptor, Sentinel
- Notes: Give with food; requires annual blood test and prescription; give year-round.

**Flea and Tick Prevention**
- Oral monthly (NexGard, Simparica, Bravecto 3-month)
- Topical monthly (Frontline Plus, Revolution)
- Apply topical products between the shoulder blades so the pet cannot lick them off.

**Core Vaccines**
- DHPP (distemper, hepatitis, parvovirus, parainfluenza): every 1–3 years after puppy series.
- Rabies: every 1–3 years depending on local regulation.
- Bordetella (kennel cough): every 6–12 months for social or boarded dogs.

### Cats

**Flea Prevention**
- Topical monthly (Revolution Plus, Frontline): apply to back of neck.
- Some oral options available; ask your vet.

**Core Vaccines**
- FVRCP (rhinotracheitis, calicivirus, panleukopenia): every 1–3 years after kitten series.
- Rabies: every 1–3 years; required by law in many areas.
- FeLV (feline leukemia): recommended for outdoor or multi-cat household cats.

## Administering Medications Safely

**Pills**
1. Conceal in a treat, pill pocket, or soft food.
2. For resistant pets, use a pill gun to place the pill at the back of the tongue.
3. Always follow with a small amount of water or food to ensure the pill reaches the stomach.

**Liquid Medications**
1. Use the provided measuring syringe for accurate dosing.
2. Insert the syringe into the corner of the mouth (not straight back) and dispense slowly.
3. Refrigerate if the label instructs; always shake suspensions before use.

**Topical Spot-On Treatments**
1. Part the fur to expose skin between the shoulder blades.
2. Apply entire contents of applicator directly to skin.
3. Keep pet dry for 24–48 hours after application.

## Medication Tracking

Log every dose with: date, time, medication name, dose amount, and who administered it. This prevents double-dosing in multi-person households and helps identify adverse reactions.

**Missed dose guidelines:**
- Within 12 hours: give as soon as remembered.
- More than 12 hours late: skip and resume the normal schedule — do not double dose.
- For antibiotics, heart medications, or seizure medications: contact your vet immediately.

## Warning Signs of Adverse Reactions

Call your veterinarian if you observe within 1–4 hours of administering medication:
- Vomiting, diarrhea, or loss of appetite
- Excessive lethargy or sudden weakness
- Facial swelling, hives, or skin redness
- Difficulty breathing or rapid heart rate
- Loss of coordination or seizure activity
