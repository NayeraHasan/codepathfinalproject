# General Pet Care and Wellness

## Building a Consistent Routine

Pets thrive on predictability. A reliable daily routine supports healthy digestion, reduces anxiety, and makes medication scheduling easier. An effective routine covers:

1. Regular feeding — same times daily
2. Exercise or interactive play
3. Medication administration (if applicable)
4. Grooming and hygiene maintenance
5. Veterinary check-ups on schedule

## Veterinary Appointments

- Annual wellness exams for healthy adult dogs and cats.
- Semi-annual exams for senior pets (7+ years).
- Emergency vet: any sudden changes in appetite, mobility, behavior, or breathing.

Keep a written or digital record of your pet's vaccines, medications, and vet visits. This is critical if you ever need emergency care at an unfamiliar clinic.

## Scheduling Best Practices

To minimize conflicts and missed tasks:
- Space different medications at least 2 hours apart unless directed otherwise by a vet.
- Schedule walks before meals (not immediately after) to reduce bloat risk in large dogs.
- Allow 30 minutes between feeding and vigorous exercise.
- Keep morning and evening routines within a consistent 30-minute window each day.
- For households with multiple pets, use separate feeding stations to prevent food aggression.

## Multi-Pet Households

Managing several pets requires extra planning:
- Individual feeding stations reduce competition and allow monitoring of each pet's intake.
- Schedule individual one-on-one attention daily for each pet.
- Use task labels (pet name in each task) to avoid confusion.
- Watch for signs of stress in pets who share space: hiding, reduced appetite, or aggression.

## Seasonal Care Adjustments

- **Summer**: Shift walks to early morning or evening to avoid heat injury; increase water availability.
- **Winter**: Protect paws from ice and de-icing salts; rinse paws after outdoor walks; consider pet-safe booties.
- **Spring/Fall**: Heighten flea and tick vigilance; watch for seasonal allergies (itching, watery eyes, sneezing).

## Emergency Preparedness

Keep readily accessible:
- Your primary vet's number and the nearest 24-hour emergency animal hospital.
- A basic pet first aid kit (gauze, antiseptic wipes, digital thermometer, emergency blanket).
- A 3-day supply of food, medications, and clean water.
- Copies of vaccination records and a recent photo of each pet.
